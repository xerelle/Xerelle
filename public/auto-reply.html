Auto-Reply

When someone messages you for the very first time, this reply gets sent back automatically — before you've had a chance to respond yourself. It's often the single biggest factor in whether she keeps talking or subscribes, so it's worth genuinely thinking through.
What makes a good auto-reply
• Ask a real question. A reply that ends with a question ("what brought you here?") is far more likely to get a response than a flat "hey" or "thanks for the message."
• Sound like you, not a bot. Generic or robotic replies ("Thank you for your message") make people disengage immediately. Warmth and personality matter here more than anywhere else.
• Keep it short. Two or three sentences reads as genuine interest. A long paragraph can feel like a sales pitch before you've even said hello.
• Don't ask for money up front. This is her free first message — the goal is to make her want to keep talking, not to push a subscription immediately.
